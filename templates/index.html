Automated Pipeline

End-to-End Diagnostic Pathology Pipeline

From Giemsa-stained microscopic blood films to quantitative infection decisions without manual operator bias.

🩸
Stage 01

Erythrocyte Patch Extraction

Thin blood smear microscopy is segmented into individual red blood cell patches, standardizing boundary geometries and stain variations.

🧬
Stage 02

Convolutional Feature Mapping

Two 32-filter Conv2D layers with 3x3 receptive fields extract chromatin textures, pigment granules, and intracellular trophozoite rings.

⚡
Stage 03

Batch-Normalized Regularization

Four BatchNormalization layers and 0.2 Dropout stabilize internal covariate shift and eliminate slide-to-slide staining variations.

📊
Stage 04

Calibrated Parasitemia Score

Dense 512 & 256 layers generate calibrated Softmax probabilities, risk tiers, and downloadable clinical pathology lab reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Understanding the deep learning classification model and clinical application.

What dataset was used to train the neural network?

The model was trained on 27,558 segmented thin blood smear cell images collected by the NIH Malaria Screener research initiative from patients at Chittagong Medical College Hospital, Bangladesh. The dataset contains 13,779 parasitized and 13,779 uninfected cells stained with Giemsa.

How does the CNN distinguish parasites from staining artifacts?

The multi-channel convolutional filters learn deep spatial patterns specific to Plasmodium life stages (chromatin rings, cytoplasm vacuole borders, and hemozoin pigments) which distinctively differ in texture and gradient distribution from dust, platelet overlaps, or precipitate artifacts.

What is the Parasitemia Index in the Batch Smear Studio?

The Parasitemia Index calculates the percentage of parasitized red blood cells across the scanned field of view ((Infected Cells / Total Cells) × 100). This provides clinicians with quantitative infection severity metrics (e.g. >2% moderate, >20% hyperparasitemia requiring intensive clinical care).
