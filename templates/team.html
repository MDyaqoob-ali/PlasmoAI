The Diagnostic Challenge

Manual examination of Giemsa-stained thick and thin blood films under light microscopy remains the clinical standard for malaria diagnosis. However, manual microscopy is labor-intensive, subject to microscopist fatigue, and prone to inter-observer variability in regions with limited experienced personnel.

⏱️

Time-Critical Urgency

In non-immune individuals, Plasmodium falciparum can progress rapidly from mild symptoms to severe malaria, organ failure, and death within 24–48 hours if untreated. Rapid, automated cell classification provides critical triage acceleration.

🎯

Deep Learning Solution

PlasmoAI employs an end-to-end convolutional neural network that automatically learns discriminative spatial features—including intracellular chromatin dots and ring-form contours—directly from segmented red blood cell patches with 95.23% accuracy.

Dataset Provenance

NIH Malaria Screener Research Dataset

27,558 Segmented Slides
Source Institution National Library of Medicine (NIH)
Clinical Partner Chittagong Medical College Hospital
Class Balance 13,779 Parasitized / 13,779 Uninfected
Staining Protocol Standard Giemsa-Stained Thin Smears

Thin blood smear slides were captured using a smartphone-adapted microscope camera across 150 P. falciparum-infected and 50 healthy patients. Red blood cell patches were segmented using level-set active contours and verified by expert microscopists at the Mahidol-Oxford Tropical Medicine Research Unit.
